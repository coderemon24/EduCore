# UX Research & Product Discovery

## Purpose
Guide the team from vague requirements to an authoritative, evidence-grounded UX foundation. UX research ensures that design and engineering decisions solve real human and business problems rather than generating speculative features.

## When to Use
- At the start of every new client project or major feature initiative (Gate 1).
- When scoping user personas, customer journeys, or navigation reorganization.
- Before writing any visual design specs or frontend code.

## Step-by-Step Workflow

### Step 1: Client & Business Objective Intake
1. Identify the core business model, commercial objectives, and key performance indicators (KPIs).
2. Clarify primary constraints: technical stack, regulatory requirements, target timeline, and operational realities.
3. Formulate the core value proposition: what exact problem does this product solve that alternatives fail at?

### Step 2: Target Audience Profiling & Persona Development
1. Define 2–3 distinct user personas (e.g., Primary Operator, Secondary Beneficiary, Executive Decision Maker).
2. For each persona, document:
   - **Role & Context**: Daily environment, device usage habits, technical proficiency.
   - **Jobs to be Done (JTBD)**: "When I [situation], I want to [action], so I can [outcome]."
   - **Pain Points & Frustrations**: What friction exists in their current manual or legacy workflow?
   - **Success Criteria**: What makes their interaction with this screen fast, error-free, and satisfying?

### Step 3: User Journey Mapping
1. Map the end-to-end user journey across 4 phases: Awareness/Entry → Orientation → Execution/Task → Confirmation/Follow-up.
2. Highlight high-friction touchpoints and specify proactive error prevention mechanisms.

### Step 4: Information Architecture (IA) & Navigation Modeling
1. Establish a clear hierarchy of content: primary navigation (top-level), secondary navigation (tabs/drawers), and contextual breadcrumbs.
2. Group related operations logically using Card Sorting principles:
   - Keep mental models aligned with user expectations rather than database schema naming.
   - Limit top-level navigation choices to 5–7 items to minimize cognitive load (Miller's Law).

## Deliverables
1. `PROJECT_BRIEF.md`: Vision, business objectives, scope boundaries, and technical constraints.
2. `UX_RESEARCH.md`: Research findings, user journey maps, and behavioral heuristics.
3. `USER_PERSONAS.md`: Detailed persona sheets with JTBD statements and pain points.
4. `INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md`: Sitemap, navigation hierarchies, and content taxonomy.

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Is every persona anchored in a real user group rather than an abstract archetype?
- [ ] Are all JTBD statements outcome-oriented rather than feature-oriented?
- [ ] Does the information architecture limit top-level choices to 7 or fewer items?
- [ ] Are cognitive load and task completion friction explicitly mitigated?
- [ ] Has the brief clearly marked what is in-scope vs out-of-scope?
